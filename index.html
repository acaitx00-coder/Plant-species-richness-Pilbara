Plant species richness and foliage cover across Pilbara monitoring plots

A desktop assessment of TERN AusPlots vegetation surveys in Western Australia

Findings

The analysis covers 35 plots, with one visit per plot between 2015-04-28 and 2016-08-09. Vouchers recorded 19 to 87 species-level taxa per plot (median 44). Acacia aptaneura had the highest mean live projected foliage cover (3.38%) and was recorded by live point intercept at 9 plots.

Survey selection and methods

The WA inventory contained 174 plots and 209 visits. The Pilbara subset contained 50 visits to 35 plots. Recorded plot dimensions were 100 x 100 m. We selected the latest visit per plot with both a species-resolved voucher and a species-resolved live foliage hit. This selects for identifiable vegetation and can omit genuinely sparse or bare sites. Review the visit audit before extending this rule to other regions.

We counted distinct species-level names in the voucher table. We retained records ranked as species, subspecies or variety, pooled infraspecific names under the supplied species name, and retained recognised phrase names at species rank. We excluded missing or placeholder names and records identified only to genus or family. Richness describes the species recorded by this method.

For each species and visit, we divided the number of distinct point positions with a live foliage hit by the number of distinct surveyed positions, including substrate positions. We required explicit false values for both dead material and within-canopy sky. Multiple contacts with the same species at one position counted once. Plot denominators ranged from 1010 to 1010 positions. Each plot received equal weight in the mean. A species with no eligible hit at an included plot contributed zero recorded cover.

Effect of survey selection

Both raw tables contained records for 15 of the 15 excluded latest visits. Of these excluded visits, 15 lacked usable standardised names in both tables, and 15 retained herbarium determinations in both. Missing standardisation limits species-level comparisons even when field observations exist.

Follow-up survey priorities

The first two species differed by only 0.06 percentage points in mean cover. Their live hits occurred in 9 and 7 of the 35 plots respectively. High mean cover can therefore reflect substantial cover at a subset of locations. The ranking alone gives little guidance on which species will dominate a newly surveyed plot.

Prioritise the 15 plots with unusable latest visits for a review of voucher identifications. Cross-check herbarium determinations against vouchers and an accepted taxonomic reference before pooling them with standardised names. Where a current baseline is required, revisit the older plots in comparable seasonal conditions and repeat both voucher collection and point intercept. Compare plots of contrasting richness within similar landforms before attributing differences to disturbance.
